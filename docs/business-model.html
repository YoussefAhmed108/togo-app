Who Pays for the Pin

Every TikTok share costs real money to turn into a place. This is what it costs, why the obvious answer doesn't work in Cairo, and the four ways someone else can pay for it.

The cache gets cheaper as you grow — that's the important part. A city has a bounded set of venues worth filming, and TikTok concentrates hard on whichever are having a moment. The fiftieth video about the same New Cairo restaurant costs one cent, not four. Cost per user falls with density instead of rising with scale.

Two caveats on the table above. The first row is measured; the second and third are projections with the hit rates stated — real numbers will arrive in the extract/cache logs within a day of traffic. And Google's Places free tier (roughly 5,000 Pro-SKU calls per month, worth confirming against current pricing) covers the first few thousand extractions outright, so early bills will run under even the optimistic row.

The rejected default

Consumer subscriptions don't work here — and it isn't just willingness to pay

The instinct is a $4.99 Pro tier. In Egypt that fails on three independent counts, any one of which would be enough:

  • Price anchoring. $4.99 is roughly EGP 240/month against local media subscriptions in the EGP 60–100 range. A place-keeping app is not priced above a music service.
  • Billing rails. Recurring card billing has thin penetration. The country runs on InstaPay, Fawry and mobile wallets — one-off rails, not subscription rails. This is infrastructure, not preference, and no amount of product work fixes it.
  • Category. Social utilities are the hardest possible sell for consumer payment anywhere. Conversion would sit under 1%, on a base that isn't large yet.

So the model has to put the bill somewhere other than the person sharing the TikTok. Fortunately Cairo F&B already spends heavily on exactly the audience this app collects.

Why ads are out. Egyptian display CPMs sit low single digits. At 50,000 monthly sessions that is tens of dollars against a several-hundred-dollar API bill — it doesn't cover its own integration, and it degrades the one screen you want people trusting.

The uncomfortable truth about the top two. Both need someone walking into cafés with a laptop. They are the highest-value options and they are a sales business, not a self-serve one. If nobody on the team will do that, affiliate is the only line above that earns anything without headcount — plan accordingly rather than assuming the dashboard sells itself.

Sequence

Density first, revenue second

These are ordered because each phase is gated on the one before it. Supply-side revenue is unsellable without users, so the early work is cost control and growth, funded out of pocket.

NOW

Survive on zero revenue

Gate: none — done this week

Caching shipped: cold shares still cost 4.2¢, repeats cost nothing, and the same venue from a new video costs a penny. Per-user import caps (10/hour, 40/day) bound the worst case at about $1.64/user/day. Watch the extract/cache hit rates and replace the projections above with real numbers.

NEXT

Wire affiliate links into recommendations

Gate: any user base at all

Earns from free users, needs no sales team, and fits the existing recommendation row without a redesign. Won't cover costs alone — it buys runway and proves venues convert, which is the argument you'll make in the next phase.

THEN

Claimed venue profiles, sold by hand

Gate: ~2,000 active users concentrated in one city

Start with the dashboard, not the ad slot. Venues pay for the numbers first; once they're logging in to check saves, promoted placement is an upsell to an existing customer instead of a cold pitch. Price in EGP, monthly, cancel anytime.

LATER

Creator Spaces as the growth engine

Gate: a working venue-side product to sell against

The creators whose videos you already ingest bring their own audience. A sponsored Space is revenue and acquisition in one motion — the only line in this doc that does both.

Expansion

What changes outside Egypt

The extraction pipeline is already market-neutral: language detection returns any ISO code rather than defaulting to Arabic, cache keys are built from whatever the model read, and each city warms its own working set independently. Nothing needs rewriting to open a second market.

The model is what changes, and it inverts. In the Gulf, Europe or North America the consumer subscription this doc rejects becomes the obvious first line — card billing works, price anchors are 5–10× higher, and a $4.99 tier at 2% conversion beats hand-selling cafés. The supply-side plan is the right answer for Cairo specifically, not a universal one.

Two things to protect while expanding: keep cache keys market-scoped so a thin new city can't dilute a warm one, and don't let venue-side commitments in Cairo constrain what the product does elsewhere.

What would break this

Four risks worth naming

  • TikTok closes the door. Extraction depends on yt-dlp reaching the video; it already retried four times on the test link. If that path closes, the caption-only fallback is materially worse at picking the right branch — and the whole differentiator narrows.
  • Density never arrives. Every supply-side option is gated on concentration in one city. Users spread thinly across Egypt are worth far less than the same number inside Cairo.
  • Google's pricing or free tier moves. It's 77% of an uncached share. The query cache blunts this, but a Places price rise still lands directly on the cost line.
  • Promoted placement erodes trust. Recommendations are the surface users are least suspicious of. Label paid slots honestly and cap their share of the row, or you spend the asset you're trying to sell.
